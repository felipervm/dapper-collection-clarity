AN INDEPENDENT EXPLORATION · NBA TOP SHOT

A collection deserves
a clearer picture.

I liked the idea of seeing fandom through a collection. So I explored Dapper’s Fandom Graph — and started asking what its numbers actually tell a collector.

A small working extension: make the source visible, explain when the ranking changes, and keep unknown scores distinct from zero.

THE QUESTION

When the data changes,
does the explanation change with it?

The graph combines collection snapshots with a separate locked-score leaderboard. A ranking service can fail while the collection still loads. That is a useful fallback — but it needs a different label.

FROM THE MOMENT TO THE MODEL

A fan sees a collection.
The interface needs to explain the numbers.

Top Shot makes a sporting moment something a fan can collect. In the Fandom Graph, that collection becomes a network of players, editions and holders.

My focus is the layer between that network and the person reading it: what is known, what is missing, and what the current view can support.

See how the explanation changes ↘
01 / MEANING
0≠—

Unknown isn’t zero.

A holder missing from the returned leaderboard has no verified score in that response. The extension shows a dash instead of assigning zero.

Why this matters +

A verified zero is a real result. An absent holder is a missing result. Preserving that distinction avoids a conclusion the response cannot support.

02 / CONTEXT
Score↔Owned

Name the view.

When the score service is unavailable, holders are ordered by Moments owned. The sidebar, coverage line and explanation say so.

Why this matters +

A fallback can be useful without being equivalent. Showing the active metric lets a collector understand why the order changed.

03 / COVERAGE

Show the limits.

Source, missing update time, partial-data flags and subset positions are visible. The graph’s holder count now uses the loaded dataset rather than a fixed 200.

Why this matters +

The source and its boundaries belong next to the number. A partial flag, missing timestamp and loaded subset all affect how the result should be read.

WHAT I WOULD MEASURE NEXT

Can a collector explain
what they’re seeing?

I would compare the original and revised views in short usability tasks: identify the ranking metric, distinguish an unknown score from zero, and explain the source’s freshness.

Support volume and retention would require internal data. This sprint demonstrates behavior under controlled responses, not improvements in those business metrics.
